# Core Research Method

## Table Of Contents

- Intake

- Planning

- Final Output

## Intake

Start with the smallest useful intake. Ask a clarification only when it changes scope, source matrix, or output.

Capture:

- objective: what decision or understanding the research should support
- audience: who will use the output
- scope: include/exclude, geography, time window, technical level
- depth: quick, standard, or deep
- output: answer, memo, table, report, briefing, JSON, or artifact
- constraints: source constraints, freshness, budget/time, sensitivity

For deep work, create a short plan before searching. For quick work, do not over-interview.

## Depth Budget

Depth is an execution choice, not a domain.

| Depth | Use when | Typical work |
|---|---|---|
| quick | The user wants a direct answer, lookup, or small comparison | 1-3 queries, 2-4 opened sources, concise answer |
| standard | The user wants useful research but not a formal report | 3-8 queries, 5-12 opened sources, structured findings |
| deep | The user asks for deep, comprehensive, exhaustive, due diligence, or report-scale research | 8-20+ queries, 12-30+ opened sources, artifacts, contradiction search, claim audit |

Escalate depth for high-stakes domains, recent facts, conflicting sources, or decisions involving money, safety, law, medicine, or reputation.

## Planning

Decompose the objective into 3-7 angles:

- definitions and scope
- current state
- evidence for the main claim
- evidence against the main claim
- stakeholder or domain-specific angle
- risks, gaps, and unknowns
- next-step implications

For important conclusions, allocate meaningful effort to disconfirming evidence. For hypothesis-driven research, classify searches as supporting, disconfirming, or neutral.

## Search And Extraction

Use the best available search/fetch tools. Search is not evidence until the source is opened or retrieved.

For each useful source, record:

- source id
- URL or stable identifier
- title and publisher
- date published or updated when available
- date accessed
- source tier from `source-policy.md`
- claims supported or contradicted
- limitations, conflicts, or access issues

Prefer specific queries over broad queries after the first pass. Use domain-specific query patterns from the loaded domain reference.

## Synthesis

Synthesize only after enough sources have been opened. Keep notes claim-centered:

- What is the claim?
- Which source supports it?
- Is that source primary, authoritative secondary, ordinary secondary, or only a lead?
- Does another source contradict it?
- Is the claim current, stale, jurisdiction-specific, or context-specific?

Use confidence labels:

- high: primary or multiple independent authoritative sources agree
- medium: credible secondary support or partial primary support
- low: thin evidence, stale source, or unresolved conflict
- unresolved: no adequate source found

## Final Output

For quick work, answer directly and list the most important sources.

For standard/deep work, include:

- research objective and scope
- key findings
- evidence and citations
- source quality notes
- conflicts or uncertainty
- open questions
- recommended next steps when useful
- audit summary: queries/sources opened/sources cited

Every answer — quick, standard, or deep — closes with an explicit confidence label (high / medium / low / unresolved) and the single biggest open uncertainty, stated up front rather than buried. If evidence is weak, say so plainly. The reader is calibrating how much weight to put on the result before they act on it; an unlabeled answer forces them to guess, and they will usually guess "confident."
