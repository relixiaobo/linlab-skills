# Layout Compiler Contract

Use this for new PPTX decks and any deck where visual layout is generated by an
agent. The model may choose the message, recipe, asset role, and density. A
deterministic compiler must own geometry, layer order, pagination, and final
global elements.

## Core Contract

- Treat the deck plan as the source of truth and PPTX as a compiled artifact.
- Choose a registered layout recipe before placing any object.
- Assign every slide a content density: `sparse`, `medium`, or `dense`.
- Assign every visual asset a role, fit, slot, and layer before insertion.
- Assign every important image intrinsic width, intrinsic height, aspect ratio,
  aspect class, crop permission, focal point, and minimum effective PPI before
  insertion.
- Never append images or extra sections to an already rendered PPTX without
  relayout and a full verification pass.
- For PPTX, use one final writer to render slide order, closing slides, page
  numbers, totals, section counters, and global marks.

## Canvas Model

Use a 16:9 canvas unless the source template says otherwise. The default canvas
is 13.333 x 7.5 inches.

- safe area: keep normal content inside left/right/top/bottom margins
- title band: reserved for section label, headline, and optional rule
- body area: all layout recipes operate inside this area
- footer zone: page numbers, source notes, logos, and global marks only
- full-bleed exception: only background or hero-media objects may touch edges

Objects outside the safe area are defects unless the recipe explicitly marks
them as bleed or intentional crop.

## Layer Order

Render layers in this order:

1. background fills and full-bleed background images
2. structural frames, rules, and quiet containers
3. primary media, charts, maps, screenshots, and diagrams
4. image overlays, scrims, callout connectors, and highlights
5. body text, labels, captions, and annotations
6. logos, page numbers, source notes, and section counters

A blank or decorative shape must not sit above an image unless it is an
intentional translucent overlay. A full-slide image above text is a defect.

## Content Density

- `sparse`: one claim, one metric cluster, one quote, or one visual proof. Do
  not place a small paragraph block in the upper-left corner of an otherwise
  empty slide.
- A title/subtitle-only slide followed by the actual table, chart, or evidence
  is a layout defect unless it is explicitly a section divider.
- `medium`: one main visual or 2-4 structured content groups.
- `dense`: table, matrix, evidence wall, or multi-part analysis. Use smaller
  type only within recipe limits; split the slide when objects become tiny or
  collide.

Sparse content should be centered, staged, paired with a visual, or converted
to a statement, metric, quote, section, or split recipe.

## Recipe Geometry Rules

### split

- Use non-overlapping text and visual rectangles.
- A right-side image requires the text column width to end before the image
  slot begins.
- Use contain-fit for screenshots, maps, and charts where text/detail matters.
- Use cover-fit only for photography or intentional editorial crops.

### feature-grid

- Maximum 4 columns in one row.
- 5-6 items must wrap to two rows or become a comparison/matrix.
- More than 6 items must paginate, group, or use evidence-wall.
- Cards must have equal structure and fixed dimensions.

### process and value-chain

- Maximum 5 nodes per horizontal row.
- More than 5 stages must wrap, paginate, or use a timeline/stacked recipe.
- Stage labels may not overflow slide edges.
- Connectors must adjust to wrapped rows instead of extending past the canvas.

### timeline

- Maximum 7 date or milestone labels in one horizontal row.
- More than 7 milestones must wrap, paginate, group by era, or become a
  vertical/stacked timeline.
- Milestone detail text below 8 pt is a defect for generated PPTX.

### table-takeaway

- Tables must fit inside the body area as native table or structured groups.
- Tables with 9+ rows and 6+ columns, 12+ rows, or 8+ columns need splitting,
  summarization, or pagination.
- Table text below 9 pt is allowed only when the table is secondary evidence
  and remains readable in a rendered thumbnail; below 8 pt is a defect.
- A slide may not be only a title for a table that appears on the next slide.

### map-callout

- Use when place, region, field, basin, market, or route matters.
- The map is the primary visual; callouts explain the claim.
- Do not use a generic world image for a specific geography unless the slide
  explicitly frames global context.

### section

- A section slide may use a full-bleed image only as the bottom background
  layer, with a contrast overlay and text above it.
- Do not insert a full-slide image after section text.
- Do not insert foreground evidence, map, chart, or screenshot media on top of
  section title/subtitle text. Convert the slide to a content recipe or create a
  separate map-callout/image-led slide.

### card components

- Use small radii unless the design system explicitly requires pill shapes.
- Text must have internal padding and must be visually attached to the card.
- Avoid giant rounded rectangles containing only a small text list.
- Do not use a separate free-floating text box over a rounded rectangle unless
  the compiler binds the text box to the card geometry.

## Asset Rules

Each asset in a deck plan should specify:

- `role`: background, hero, right-evidence, left-evidence, map, chart, logo,
  thumbnail, callout, or accepted decorative asset
- `fit`: contain, cover, stretch, or original
- `slot`: recipe slot such as visual-right, hero, map-primary, logo-footer
- `layer`: background, image, overlay, text, logo, or folio
- `intrinsicWidthPx` and `intrinsicHeightPx`: source image dimensions
- `aspectRatio` and `aspectClass`: panoramic, wide, landscape, square,
  portrait, tall, or unknown
- `cropAllowed`: false for logos, maps, charts, screenshots, tables, and other
  detail-bearing evidence
- `focalPoint`: center, face-left, field-label, basin-center, or another
  concise focus hint
- `minEffectivePpi`: minimum resolution after scaling or cropping

Image insertion is a layout event. It must trigger recipe selection or relayout;
it must not be a post-hoc object placed over existing slide content.

Fit policy:

- Use `contain` for logos, maps, screenshots, charts, tables, and evidence where
  detail or text must remain visible.
- Use `cover` for photography, hero media, or background images only when
  cropping is acceptable and the focal point remains visible.
- Avoid `stretch` except for explicitly accepted abstract textures or
  non-informational backgrounds.
- If the asset aspect does not match the target slot, change the slot or recipe,
  paginate, or choose another asset instead of distorting the image.

## Required QA Gates

Before delivery, verify:

- no normal object extends outside the slide or safe area
- no text box overlaps a picture unless the recipe explicitly defines an
  overlay with contrast treatment
- no foreground picture collides with section divider text
- no blank shape covers a picture
- no full-slide picture sits above text
- no picture is distorted by a display box that conflicts with its effective
  image aspect
- no important picture is scaled below its minimum effective PPI
- no detail-bearing picture is cropped without an explicit crop permission
- no grid/process row exceeds recipe limits
- no timeline row exceeds recipe limits or uses tiny milestone text
- no table extends outside the slide/body area or requires tiny unreadable type
- no sparse title/stub slide appears immediately before its actual evidence
- no stale `current / total` page numbers remain
- no closing, thanks, or Q&A slide appears before later content unless the deck
  plan explicitly defines a mid-deck close
- rendered representative slides match the intended composition

Fail the deck or mark it as needing repair when these checks find unresolved
issues.
