# Institutional Editorial

Use for policy, institutional history, executive narrative, and ideas that need gravity without becoming ceremonial.

## Character

- Editorial contrast: serif display type, disciplined sans-serif evidence, and asymmetrical margins.
- Warm white and charcoal carry most of the page; red marks the governing thesis and teal marks a counterpoint.
- Large quotations or archival assets should remain unframed and materially connected to the argument.

## Signature Moves

- Open sections with a short thesis and one primary-source fragment.
- Use vertical chronology and marginal annotations for historical or regulatory sequences.
- Let one red rule establish hierarchy; avoid decorating every block with the accent.

## Adaptation

Derive the recurring device from the subject: a policy margin, annotated archive, governance stack, or institutional timeline. Validate CJK serif wrapping before committing to the direction.
