## Use When

Use for academic, scientific, medical, technical, or scholarly research: literature review, related work, systematic review, papers, citations, DOI, PubMed, arXiv, Semantic Scholar, or research gaps.

## Source Matrix

Prefer:

1. original papers and publisher/DOI pages
2. PubMed, Semantic Scholar, arXiv, bioRxiv, medRxiv, Crossref
3. official conference/journal pages and proceedings
4. systematic reviews and meta-analyses
5. clinical trial registries or official datasets when relevant
6. reputable scholarly summaries only as context

Avoid relying on paper snippets alone. If a paper matters, open the abstract, methods, results, and limitations where available.

## Method

1. Clarify research question, year range, field, and output.
2. Choose a framework when useful:
   - PICO for clinical intervention questions
   - SPIDER for qualitative/social research
   - Decomposition for technology/method questions
3. Run a broad reconnaissance search to map terminology.
4. Split into 4-5 sub-areas.
5. Search each sub-area with query variants.
6. Identify repeat-hit papers, recurring authors/groups, review papers, and recent frontier papers.
7. Deep-read the most relevant 3-10 papers depending on depth.
8. Follow citations for central papers when useful.

## Quality Signals

Evaluate:

- relevance to the exact question
- study design and sample/data quality
- peer review status versus preprint
- venue or publisher
- citation count normalized by age
- whether it is original research, review, or commentary
- limitations and conflicts of interest
- whether claims are supported by results rather than abstract framing

## Output Shape

Include:

- literature map
- priority reading order
- paper table
- what the evidence shows
- methods and limitations
- disagreements or gaps
- useful search strings
- bibliography or DOI list when possible
